## How rows were made

- **Synthetic look-alikes:** dnstwist (typo, ASCII/Unicode homoglyph, bitsquat), ail-typo-squatting (hyphen insertion), and our own generators in `doppel/generator/own.py` (twin letters from `data/lookalikes/ascii_twins.yaml`, Cyrillic/Greek homoglyphs from Unicode confusables incl. whole-script, combosquat from the registry lure words, ending swap, level-squat, trust-signal mimicry, free hosting, soundsquat). The targeted generators are kept first, and dnstwist fills the rest, up to 400 per brand and technique.
- **Dropped:** anything an attacker couldn't register (under a restricted registry or a closed brand TLD), anything genuine or on a brand's user-content host, and anything that is actually a popular real domain (that becomes a hard negative). Drops: genuine 12.
- **Real phishing (`test_real`):** PhiUSIIL phishing rows whose host P2 links to one of our brands. **Caveat:** they were *selected* with P2's name signals, so name-level recall on this set is not a fair metric; use it for the later stages, real-world closeness and the demo.
- **Hard negatives:** Majestic Million + PhiUSIIL legitimate domains that resemble a brand (P2 signal). Popular, established domains are presumed not phishing. Many are brand affiliates (icicilombard.com, hdfclife.com) or other companies with similar names (SBI Holdings Japan, US state banks). Per brand: google 357, income_tax 3, sbi 15, hdfc 6, paytm 4, icici 9, india_post 4.
- **Genuine:** every registry family domain, its `www.` form, and the subdomains named in registry notes.
- **Benign:** 10,000 random names from the NRD snapshot with no P2 brand signal. Unverified: some may be phishing for *other* brands, but none imitate ours by name.

## Splits

- Held-out brands (icici, india_post) appear **only** in `test_unseen`/`test_real`.
- Other rows: a hash of the candidate decides (70/15/15 train/dev/test_seen; benign 60/15/15/10, the last 10% to test_unseen so that split has negatives).
- `test_seen`, `test_unseen` and `test_real` are **never used for tuning**.

## Known limitations

- Synthetic look-alikes are *designed* imitations, not registered domains; the label means "imitates the brand", not "is malicious".
- dnstwist's Unicode homoglyphs are mostly accented Latin letters (pāyţm), which UTS-39 skeletons don't fold, hence the low P2 number for T3. Mixed-script Cyrillic look-alikes come from our own generator.
- T12 (soundsquat) is small: a rule set plus a simple phonetic key, not a full phonetic algorithm.
- Hard negatives are presumed legitimate because they're popular; a popular compromised site would be mislabelled.
